# Final research review

Independent reading of Paper 1 after the observation-contract checks. This is not an Apart score and not a prediction of one. Estimates are 1–5 on the three public criteria. They are not inflated to reach 12/15.

## Why innovation is not 4

A 4 would require an original contribution other researchers could build on, beyond a careful application of known ideas. The paper's real contribution is empirical and narrow. A prompted tool name carries a bit when it is visible. Rewriting that name and deleting the event both remove the tested distinction when the assignment stays. Canonicalization shows no utility advantage over event deletion. Hiding the receiver's assignment, on the same seeds, is followed by duplicate claims on three seeds and not on seed 12.

The contract checker makes that split executable. It is observational equivalence plus a declared projection, at one prompt boundary, for two synthetic tools. Those ideas are old. The module is specific to this assignment heading and these function names. A reviewer who adopts it gains a test for this environment, not a method that finds task-relevant state in another system. That is not enough for an innovation score of 4. Calling it a runtime observation contract does not add a phenomenon the episodes did not already show.

Execution is a 4 because the cohorts are separated, request logs exist for the later episodes, negative results are kept, and the offline checks include cases that must not pass. It is not a 5. One model, two draws per holdout cell, synthetic equivalent tools, an unrepaired codebook gap in the original 108, and seed 12 all remain. Presentation is a 4. The question, the policies, and the limits can be followed. The manuscript is dense because it now carries three cohorts and a checker, which is the cost of not pooling them.

## Strongest contribution

Tool-execution metadata and legitimate task state are different objects in this runtime, and only the second one tracks the coordination failure in the direct comparison. T4 withholds the name and keeps the assignment. T3 withholds both. The receiver's duplicate claims line up with T3 on seeds 11, 13, and 14. The checker fails T3's receiver on task-state preservation and passes T4 on both properties, using the prompt the code returns.

## What the checker establishes

On seeds 11 and 2, for one equivalent successful claim, the returned observations match the policy table: T1 fails identity confidentiality and passes task-state preservation; T2 and T4 pass both; T3 passes identity and fails task state for the receiver, and passes task state for the third agent. The audit log still contains the real tool name, including on a retry and on a failed second claim. A nested name, a placeholder assignment section, and unequal tasks that have been wiped out of the observation do not pass.

## What it cannot establish

It cannot establish noninterference, closure of other channels, or that the model will coordinate whenever the assignment section is present. Seed 12 is the counterexample already in the episodes: the section was absent and the assignment still matched T4. It cannot establish utility noninferiority. It cannot establish that harmful collusion was prevented. The study never ran a harmful coalition. It cannot establish that two tools are safe to canonicalize merely because a caller wants them to be. Equivalence has to be declared, and the declaration fails when the assignment projections differ. Costs, permissions, and other side effects are outside the projection.

## Remaining weaknesses

The tools are synthetic equivalents. The protected channel is a codebook the agents were given. Holdout cells have two draws. The original signaling prompts do not contain the codebook text. T2's seed-14 dependence of 0.5 is one action under a bit-independent observation, which is reported and is still a small sample. The contract parser will miss an assignment that this environment starts rendering in another format.

## Is the manuscript ready?

Yes, for the claim it now makes. The exploratory cohort, the holdout, the eight T3 episodes, the canonicalization negative result, the seed-12 exception, and the logging gap are still in the paper. The contract section states what was checked and what a pass does not mean.

Further episodes are not required to submit that claim. They would be required to justify an innovation score of 4. A second environment, or a tool pair whose semantic difference is not the assignment id the authors already named, would be a different paper. Adding more seeds of the same synthetic pair would improve execution. It would not, by itself, make the contract a general method.
